## 2. Where Orbit is genuinely differentiated

These are the rows where **no live competitor scores ✅** — Orbit's defensible wedge:

1. **Partial rotation (bond + novelty)** — nobody does this. Event-first apps reset every event (no bonds); community platforms keep groups static (cliques form). Orbit is the only one keeping ~half and swapping ~half.
2. **Reliability / reputation score** — no competitor tracks and rewards attendance reliability as a first-class system.
3. **Group health monitoring + at-risk nudges** — no competitor proactively detects a dying group and intervenes before the death spiral.
4. **The *combined* system** — several apps have *one* piece (Timeleft has matching + app-run events; Geneva has persistent identity). None combine matching + persistence + rotation + rotating ownership + fallback + health + reputation into one self-sustaining machine.

## 3. Where Orbit must match table stakes (not differentiators, but required)
- Cross-platform, in-app chat, safety (verify/report/block), vetted venues, free tier, clear venue details. These aren't wedges — they're the price of entry, and several competitors already do them well.

## 4. Where competitors are ahead today (honest gaps to close)
| Gap | Who's ahead | Orbit's plan |

## 5. Positioning statement

> **For** people who want real, lasting friendships (not another dead chat thread or a one-off dinner), **Orbit** is a social club app that **matches you into a small group, keeps you bonding through partial rotation, and can't fall apart when one person quits** — because ownership rotates and the app runs the logistics. **Unlike** Timeleft (great night, no continuity), Meetup (dies with the organizer), or Bumble BFF (matches that never meet), Orbit is the only one that is simultaneously **continuous and self-sustaining.**
